# Curated puzzle sets: research

Do puzzle people share single puzzles or sets, how are good sets made, and what should inkit build?
Companion to [research-lists.md](research-lists.md) (search as the list) and
[tutorial-sequences.md](tutorial-sequences.md) (how Slate orders a week).

Researched October 2026, signed out, public pages only, standard web search. Raw notes are in
`references/lists/curation-notes.md` (local only). Reddit pages could not be fetched, so Reddit
evidence here comes from mirrors or is missing. Puzzle Square JP was not contacted, as asked.

## The hypothesis

> Two uses: (1) play the thing I just browsed (a search, a creator's puzzles); (2) true curation,
> a carefully sequenced set, which is what drives real sharing.

## (a) Verdict: partly right

**Right:**
- The two uses are real and different. Nobody in the puzzle world titles or shares a search. When
  people share a set, it has a name, an order and a reason.
- Careful sets are what get passed on. The Logic Masters "Beginner's Guide to Variant Sudokus" is
  eight tiny puzzles in one post; commenters say they'll pass it to family and students.
  GMPuzzles, Conceptis, Krazydad and Nikoli all sell or post puzzles as ramps.

**Needs changing:**
1. **The single puzzle is still the main unit of sharing.** SudokuPad links, puzz.link URLs, a Logic
   Masters entry, a Windmill link, a Baba level code: each is one puzzle. Sets are rarer and
   matter more.
2. **Most curated sets are the curator's own puzzles.** A setter's series (Tockman's "seal" series,
   juggler's guide, Blobz's 19-puzzle Patreon set, a GMPuzzles week by one author). Sets of other
   people's puzzles are made mainly by **editors** (Cracking the Cryptic's apps, Logic Masters'
   beginner lists, Baba's Featured Levels, "What The Witness?") and by **teachers** (Lichess coaches).
   We found few ordinary players making playlists of other people's puzzles.
3. **There is a third use: practice.** Repeat a fixed set until it's fast (chess's Woodpecker
   method), or a teacher assigns a set and checks who solved what. Lichess users ask for both.
4. **A good set is a work in its own right.** It is posted once, commented on as a whole, often
   finished in one go, and sometimes has its own answer (a solution code, a hunt's meta). It is
   not just a pointer to puzzles.

So: keep search-as-list for (1), free and light. Build (2) as a published *set*, mostly made by
creators from their own puzzles, open to other people's puzzles with credit. Plan for (3) later.

## (b) What the community wants

### 1. A ramp, labelled

- GMPuzzles: "Puzzles get progressively harder throughout the week"; Easy on Monday/Tuesday, Hard
  on Friday/Saturday. Focus weeks take one or two styles from easy to hard.
  ([about](https://gmpuzzles.com/blog/?p=437))
- Conceptis runs very easy on Monday to hard on Sunday ([listing](https://tsscontent.ca/?p=874)).
  Krazydad's book number is the difficulty (book 100 harder than book 1, across volumes).
  Nikoli's *Original Sudoku* is arranged "Easy" to "Very Hard".
- Beast Academy's puzzle books go from "straightforward starters to diabolical stumpers" per type;
  its online Puzzle Lab is type → puzzle sets ([help](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1890360)).
- Players notice when a ramp is wrong: Nikoli reviewers found some "very hard" easier than
  "medium"; a Lichess trainer says even the easiest level is too hard for beginners
  ([forum](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/suggestions-for-extra-functions-for-trainers)).
  Patrick Traynor's first playtests showed "the difficulty curve was way too steep", so he added
  levels ([interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-patrick-traynor-s-i-patrick-s-parabox-i-)).

### 2. One idea at a time, with a line of text

- juggler's guide on Logic Masters: one variant per puzzle, "tiny 4x4 or 6x6 grids", each with a
  short rule note and a SudokuPad link, all in one post
  ([LMD](https://logic-masters.de/Raetselportal/Raetsel/zeigen.php?id=000GOJ)).
- Lichess studies: one puzzle per chapter, a comment to set it up, a hint behind a button, a
  custom message for a known wrong move ([blog](https://lichess.org/blog/WtDErSQAADJfhJJs/interactive-lessons)).
- Traynor treats ordering as a skill: insert, reorder, delete, make optional
  ([GDC 2024 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Traynor_Patrick_SystemCentricPuzzle.pdf)).

### 3. Credit and consent

- Cracking the Cryptic asks each setter how a puzzle may be used: video only, community post, or
  Patreon. "Use it how you choose" covers all ([submissions](https://www.crackingthecryptic.com/submissions)).
- Their app sells packs of "handcrafted and curated puzzles from the world's best Sudoku
  constructors", each credited ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1629992934)).
- "What The Witness?" says its puzzle sets were "created and shared by game fans, and we collected
  them", many from the WitCup contest ([RAWG](https://rawg.io/games/what-the-witness)).
- Logic Masters' beginner lists are "put together manually" by the site, not by users
  ([help](https://logic-masters.de/Raetselportal/Hilfe/suchen.php?chlang=en)). Tags too are set by
  admins.

We found no public fight over curating someone's puzzles with credit. Fights are about copying
without credit or for money (Nikoli against an app in 2010). Curating with a link and a name
looks accepted; selling or paywalling someone's puzzle needs their yes.

### 4. Progress, and seeing others' progress

- A Lichess coach of 10-year-olds asks to "set specific puzzles into a group to assign to the
  kids", with results "as a matrix showing ticks and crosses"
  ([forum](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/tagbuild-a-list-of-puzzles-to-assign-to-a-lichess-class-and-review-group-results)).
- Cracking the Cryptic's Classic Sudoku app unlocks puzzles with stars earned by solving.
- The Patreon challenge of January 2025: 19 puzzles by one setter, one theme, to solve in order,
  rated 1–4 webs ([write-up](https://brainwagon.org/blog/cracking-the-cryptic-january-2025-patreon-challenge/)).
- Logic Masters comments on a set are mostly solve times for the whole set.

### 5. Loop a fixed set

- The Woodpecker method: the same set, again and again, faster each time
  ([Lichess blog](https://lichess.org/@/CheckRaiseMate/blog/the-woodpecker-method/m4cW82R0)).
- In a Lichess AMA a player asked to replay solved puzzles, calling it small "but it would be game
  changing". The team said they had removed spaced repetition because most users thought it was a
  bug ([mirror](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/chess/comments/1j0whqu/lichess_team_ama/mfnhxf8/?context=3)).
- Another asks for Puzzle Storm on "your own database" of puzzles, shareable
  ([forum](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/puzzle-storm-upload-your-own-database)).

### 6. Find good sets

- A new sudoku site pitched itself against SudokuPad's "missing features like a searchable puzzle
  list" ([LMD forum](https://forum.logic-masters.de/showthread.php?tid=2262)). We could not confirm
  any SudokuPad collections feature.
- Logic Masters' front page offers fixed lists like "Easy and nice" beside types and authors.
  Baba Is You has a curated "Featured levels" list beside level codes
  ([GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2020/12/incredible-puzzle-game-baba-is-you-gains-a-level-editor-and-easy-sharing)).
- A Windmill setter posted a Fez-themed series and stopped: "not a very long series, but I kind of
  ran out of ideas" ([r/thewindmill mirror](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/thewindmill/comments/aegflh/fez_puzzle)).
  Small series by one person are common and often unfinished.

## (c) Kinds of curated sets

| kind | example | who makes it | authors | order | needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Series** (open, grows) | GMPuzzles week, Tockman's seal series, Slate's week, Conceptis Mon→Sun | one creator | own | by date; ramp across the week | append over time, a name, "day 2 of 5", follow |
| **Lesson** | juggler's guide, Witness tree row, Lichess study | creator or teacher | own, sometimes mixed | strict, one idea per step | intro, a note per puzzle, Next, short boards |
| **Pack / book** | Intro to GMPuzzles (12×21), Krazydad books, CtC app packs, Baba levelpacks | creator or editor | own or credited mix | ramp within each type | title, sections by type, difficulty labels, progress, resume |
| **Challenge / hunt** | CtC Patreon set (19, in order), puzzle hunts with a meta | creator or team | own | strict, sometimes locked | theme, "in order", a final answer, completion time |
| **Round / contest** | WPC and GP rounds, WitCup | organisers | mixed | fixed | time limit, points, results table, instructions |
| **Best of / picks** | LMD "Easy and nice", Baba Featured, What The Witness | editors | mixed | loose | credit, editor's line, kept short |
| **Practice / homework** | Woodpecker set, Lichess coach's homework | player or teacher | mixed | any | loop, per-puzzle results, assign to a group |

What runs through all of them: a title, an order the maker chose, credit, and a way to see how far
you are. Notes per puzzle matter for lessons and picks. Time and answers matter only for
contests and hunts.

## (d) What to change in inkit's lists

### 1. Two things, named differently

- **Search lists** (research-lists.md, system B): stay as designed. Ephemeral, no storage, the
  "‹ 3 of 41 ›" bar and Next after a solve. This covers "play what I browsed". Sharing is copying
  the URL.
- **Sets**: a new published thing. Called *set*, as GMPuzzles, Beast Academy and puzzle contests
  say. Not *collection* (that word is taken by profiles and studios) and not *list* (that's the
  search).

Linear does the same: views match by filter, initiatives are picked by hand, and they are named
differently.

### 2. What a set is

- **Fields:** title, intro (a few lines), ordered puzzles, an optional note per puzzle, optional
  section headings (for a pack with several types, or a lesson with sub-ideas, as in the Witness's
  sawtooth), and the creator.
- **Size:** 2 to 30 puzzles at first. Lichess caps studies at 64 chapters; most sets we saw were
  5 to 25.
- **Draft, then publish**, like a puzzle. A published set's order and members don't change, so a
  shared link and everyone's progress stay true. Fixing a typo in the intro or a note is fine.
- **Open sets** for series: a set can be marked open. Puzzles can then only be added at the end;
  nothing is removed or moved. The maker closes it when done. This is Slate's week and any
  creator's weekly series.
- **URL:** `/s/<id>` landing page (title, intro, cards, Start or Continue, "5 of 8 solved");
  playing it is `/g/<id>?s=<setId>`, which shows the same bar as a search list, with the set's
  title in it.
- **Storage:** `sets (id, creator_id, title, intro, open, status, published_at)` and
  `set_items (set_id, game_id, pos, note, section)`. Notes and sections need rows; the `id:` query
  text from research-lists.md can't hold them.

### 3. Other people's puzzles

- A set may include any published puzzle. Each card and the bar show that puzzle's author. The
  landing page says "Puzzles by @wyatt, @rima and Slate".
- A puzzle's page lists the public sets it is in ("In: Akari, first week"), which sends players
  to the curator and credit back to the author.
- The author can remove their puzzle from someone else's set. It then drops from the set like a
  deleted puzzle (skipped, counted out, no placeholder).
- A setting, on by default: "Others can put my puzzles in sets."
- Sets are free to play. If inkit ever charges for sets, only the author's own puzzles may go in
  a paid one (Cracking the Cryptic asks setters before paywalling).
- AI puzzles are labelled AI inside sets as everywhere else.

### 4. Playing a set

- **Resume:** Start opens the first unsolved puzzle. The bar reads "Akari, first week · 3 of 8".
- **Next after a solve** goes to the next puzzle in order, solved or not.
- **No locks at first.** Order is a strong suggestion, as on Brilliant. A per-set "in order"
  switch (next opens after a solve) can come later for challenges.
- **Done:** solving the last unsolved puzzle shows "Set done" with the time over the set.
- **Play again:** one button resets your progress in that set only (Woodpecker), keeping the old
  pass's time to compare against.
- Signed out, progress is kept in the browser, as solves are now.

### 5. Where sets show

- **Profile:** a Sets tab.
- **Feed:** a published set is one card in subscribers' feeds, not one per puzzle. An open set
  posts once when started; its new puzzles appear as usual with the set's name on them.
- **Explore:** a short row of picked sets ("Start here" per type), chosen by admins, like Logic
  Masters' beginner lists and Baba's Featured levels. Ordinary sets are found from profiles and
  puzzle pages, not ranked, at first.
- **Puzzle pages:** "In: …" with up to three sets.

### 6. Slate and the other AI creators

- Each Slate week is an open set: created Monday with the first puzzle, one added each weekday,
  closed after Friday. Title from the week's subject. The intro is Slate's one-line voice, no
  rules.
- The bar on Wednesday's puzzle reads "Week 41 · 3 of 5". A late reader gets the whole lesson in
  order from any day's post.
- The week's step types (introduce, contrast, trap, check, combine) are notes for us, not shown.
- Other personas with a weekly theme can do the same. Batches already know the week, so the
  set can be made in `puzzles/ai/week.ts`.
- Later, Slate's past weeks per symbol make a natural "Start here" pick for each type.

### 7. What to drop or change in the mockups

- **Drop the Keep tray** (`06-keep`, `06b-kept-tray`, `06c-kept-share`). It builds an untitled
  `id:` list of other people's puzzles. That is the one kind of set we found little demand for,
  and the people who do hand-pick (teachers, editors) want a title, notes and progress. Its job
  moves to "Add to a set" on a puzzle, which adds to a draft set.
- **Demote the frozen share** (`04-share-frozen`). Keep `before:` working in the URL, but Share
  on a search can just copy the link. Add "Make a set from these" next to it, which makes a
  draft set from the results in their current order.
- **Keep** the bar (`01`), Next after a solve (`02`, `02b`) and the search list (`03`). Sets reuse
  them with a title.
- **Change** the list page (`05`): it becomes the set's landing page (`/s/<id>`), with an intro,
  credits, notes and Continue. A search keeps its results page.
- The `id:` syntax can stay for links and for admins, but nothing in the UI needs to make it.

### 8. Not now

- Time limits, points and results tables (contests). Worth it only if inkit runs contests.
- A meta or final answer across a set (hunts). inkit checks each puzzle; a set-level answer
  would be a new kind of puzzle.
- Classes and per-student matrices. The Lichess request is clear, but it needs groups. A set
  plus "who solved these" for the set's maker could come first.
- Ranking or trending sets.
- Copy a set to edit your own.

### Phases

1. **Sets for creators' own puzzles.** Tables, a set editor (pick from your published puzzles,
   order, title, intro, notes), `/s/<id>`, the bar with the set's title, resume, Set done, the
   Sets tab, one feed card.
2. **Slate's weeks as open sets.** Append-only; the batch makes the set.
3. **Other people's puzzles.** "Add to a set" on any puzzle, credit on cards, "In: …" on puzzle
   pages, the author's remove and the opt-out setting. "Make a set from these" on search.
   Drop the Keep tray.
4. **Picks.** Admin-picked sets on Explore and type pages.
5. **Practice.** Play again with times per pass; later, the maker sees how many players solved
   each puzzle in the set.

## Gaps

- Reddit (r/sudoku, r/puzzles, r/TheWitness) could not be fetched; we found no thread to cite on
  how often players share sets there.
- No evidence either way on SudokuPad collections, Windmill's puzzle lists or feature requests.
- Puzzle Baron, Simon Tatham's collection and Taiji were not checked. From memory, Tatham's
  games share single puzzles by game ID and have no sets.
- Nikoli's pencil-puzzle books (not sudoku) and puzz.link's database ordering weren't confirmed.
- Lichess AMA quotes come from a Reddit mirror and may differ slightly from the original.

## Decisions (October 10, 2026)

- **Navigation while playing a set or a search: V2, arrangement A.** A bar under the board: Prev on the left, Next on the right, and in the middle the context (set or search) with progress. A set shows a thin progress bar ("4 of 8 solved", a notch at this puzzle's place). A search shows "3 of 41". Mockups: `lists-mockups/30-v2-*`.
- **The puzzle's description goes on the paper,** at the left of the paper's top strip, opposite undo, hint and reset. Curators' notes don't appear on the puzzle page.
  - The color pots (puzzles with regions or paint) move under the board, next to where Sudoku's digit pad already sits. The things you play with go under the board, and the top-left is always free.
  - Descriptions today average 78 characters, longest 154. Limit new ones to 160. The strip grows to fit: up to 2 lines on desktop, 3 on a phone.
  - The description leaves the page header, so it isn't shown twice.
  - "Solved!" and mistake messages pop up under the strip, so they don't collide with it.
